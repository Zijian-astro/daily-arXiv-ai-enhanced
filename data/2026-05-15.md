### [10] [The Demographics of Sagittarius A* X-ray Flares over 25 Years with Chandra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13962)
*Zach Sumners,Nicole M. Ford,Daryl Haggard,Joseph M. Michail,Joey Neilsen,Michael A. Nowak,Mayura Balakrishnan,Sophia Sánchez-Maes,Sebastiano D. von Fellenberg,S. P. Willner,Sera Markoff,Howard A. Smith,Joseph L. Hora*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: Analysis of 6.8 Ms of Chandra monitoring over 25 years yields a catalog of 100 Sgr A* X-ray flares (18 new, including the second brightest ever observed), with luminosities 4–575 × 10³³ erg s⁻¹. Results confirm a spectral hardening with brightness (Γ ∼3→2), strengthen correlations between duration, fluence, and count rate, and provide a resource for studying flare mechanisms.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To construct the most complete X-ray flare catalog of Sgr A* from Chandra's 25-year dataset, enabling systematic study of flare properties and physical mechanisms.

Method: Systematic analysis of 6.8 Ms of Chandra monitoring data over 25 years; spectral modeling to derive photon indices and correlations; statistical analysis of flare duration, fluence, and count rate.

Result: 100 flares identified (18 new), luminosities ∼4–575 × 10³³ erg s⁻¹; spectral index hardens from Γ∼3 to 2 with increasing brightness; correlations between duration, fluence, and maximum count rate are strengthened.

Conclusion: The results reflect variations in the underlying particle distribution producing weak vs. strong flares; the catalog provides a rich archive for future observational and numerical studies of Sgr A*'s flaring physics.

Abstract: We present the Chandra 25-year Sagittarius A* (Sgr A*) X-ray flare catalog: a systematic analysis of 6.8 Ms of Sgr A* monitoring spanning the Chandra X-ray Observatory's mission lifetime. This is the most complete Chandra Sgr A* X-ray flare catalog to date, consisting of 100 flares with 2$-$10 keV unabsorbed luminosities ranging from $\sim$ 4$-$575 $\times 10^{33}$ erg s$^{-1}$. 18 flares are reported for the first time, including the second brightest Sgr A* flare observed by Chandra. The expanded dataset supports previous indications of a correlation between X-ray flare hardness and luminosity. Spectral modeling corroborates this finding, showing a change in the X-ray spectral index, from $Γ\sim 3$ to 2 with increasing flare brightness. Previously-established correlations between flare duration, fluence, and maximum count rate are strengthened via the greater sample size. These results likely reflect variations in the underlying particle distribution that produce weak and strong flares, and the new catalog serves as a rich archive for ongoing observational and numerical investigations into the physical mechanisms responsible for producing Sgr A*'s X-ray flares.

### [11] [Infrared Line Diagnostics Fail to Constrain Sgr A*'s UV Output](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13990)
*Mayura Balakrishnan,Sebastiano D. von Fellenberg,Daryl Haggard,Joseph M. Michail,Nicole M. Ford,Joseph L. Hora,Laurent Loinard,Sera Markoff,Joey Neilsen,Giacomo Principe,Nadeen B. Sabha,Howard A. Smith,Zach Sumners,Shuo Zhang*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: JWST/MIRI observations of Sgr A*'s 0.3 arcsec region find no mid-infrared emission-line variability from flares, consistent with long light-crossing and recombination timescales suppressing observable responses; IR lines trace steady-state radiation, not individual flaring events.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To constrain the unobservable UV emission of Sgr A* (obscured by extreme extinction A_V ~ 30 mag) by searching for mid-infrared emission-line variability driven by its frequent X-ray flares.

Method: JWST/MIRI time-resolved spectroscopy of the central 0.3 arcsec region around the Galactic Center, comparing observed line variability of [Fe II] 5.34 µm, [Ne II] 12.813 µm, [Fe II] 17.936 µm, and [S III] 18.713 µm against CLOUDY photoionization models with UV flare luminosities L_UV = 10^32–10^39 erg s⁻¹ in a dense medium.

Result: No statistically significant variability was detected in any mid-infrared line. The lack of variability is consistent with physical conditions in the extended line-emitting gas: light-crossing timescales of ~0.1–10 days and recombination/cooling timescales much longer than flare durations suppress observable responses. Predicted emission is continuum-dominated, with even the brightest lines intrinsically weak and broadened by ~10³ km s⁻¹, reducing contrast.

Conclusion: Infrared emission lines trace a steady-state radiation field rather than individual flaring events; IR line diagnostics cannot constrain the instantaneous UV flux of Sgr A*.

Abstract: Sgr A*, the 4 x 10^6 solar-mass supermassive black hole at the Galactic Center, exhibits frequent flaring with X-ray luminosities of L_X ~ 10^35--10^36 erg s^-1, while its ultraviolet (UV) emission remains unconstrained due to extreme extinction (A_V ~ 30 mag). We use JWST/MIRI time-resolved spectroscopy of the central Galactic Center's 0.3 arcsec region to search for mid-infrared emission-line variability driven by Sgr A* flares, comparing the results to CLOUDY photoionization models spanning flare luminosities of L_UV = 10^32--10^39 erg s^-1 in a dense medium. We detect no statistically significant variability in any mid-infrared line, including [Fe II] 5.34 micron, [Ne II] 12.813 micron, [Fe II] 17.936 micron, and [S III] 18.713 micron. Despite expectations of a flare-driven response, we show that the lack of variability is consistent with the physical conditions in the spatially extended line-emitting gas, where light-crossing timescales of ~0.1--10 days and recombination and cooling timescales much longer than the flare timescale suppress any observable response to individual flares. We further find that the predicted emission is continuum dominated and that even the brightest lines are intrinsically weak and broadened by velocities of order 10^3 km s^-1, reducing their contrast against the continuum and limiting their detectability. Extending the analysis to higher-ionization mid-infrared and near-infrared lines does not improve sensitivity. These results demonstrate that infrared emission lines trace a steady-state radiation field rather than individual flaring events, and therefore infrared line diagnostics cannot be used to constrain the instantaneous UV flux of Sgr A*.

### [12] [Not All Who Wander Are Lost: Early Excess Demographics in the Volume-limited ZTF DR2 SN Ia Sample](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14437)
*César Rojas-Bravo,Ning-Chen Sun,Mathew Smith,Chun Chen,Xiaohan Chen,Zexi Niu,Anyu Wang,Zi-Yang Wang,Yi-Han Zhao,Jifeng Liu*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: This paper presents a systematic search for early-time flux excesses in Type Ia supernovae using ZTF DR2 data, identifying 42 early-excess and 110 no-excess events from an initial sample of 1547 SNe Ia. Early-excess events show significantly larger SALT2 stretch (x₁), stronger r-band secondary-maximum flux, bluer local (g−z) colors, and lower local stellar mass, connecting early excesses to SN Ia diversity.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Early-time flux excesses in SNe Ia offer unique insights into progenitor systems and explosion mechanisms, but demographic studies have been limited by small sample sizes. This work aims to conduct a systematic, large-scale demographic study of early-excess events.

Method: Using ZTF g- and r-band light curves from a volume-limited sample (z<0.06) of 1547 SNe Ia from ZTF DR2, the authors identify early-excess candidates shortly after explosion time. Conservative coverage and quality requirements are applied to construct reliable "excess" (bump) and "no-excess" (no-bump) catalogs. Two-sample statistical tests quantify differences in SN and host environment parameters between the two populations.

Result: Final catalogs contain 42 early-excess and 110 no-excess events. Early-excess events have significantly larger SALT2 stretch x₁ (7.91σ) and larger r-band secondary-maximum flux (6.25σ), while color c differences are weak (0.57σ). Early-excess events also favor bluer local (g−z) colors (3.41σ) and lower local stellar mass (2.73σ).

Conclusion: Early excesses are strongly connected to SN Ia diversity, particularly light-curve properties like stretch and secondary-maximum flux, and are associated with bluer, lower-mass local environments. This motivates further analyses with upcoming larger samples.

Abstract: Early-time flux excesses in Type Ia supernovae (SNe~Ia) offer a unique insight into their progenitor systems and explosion mechanisms. Although individual early-excess events and larger searches have been reported, demographic studies remain limited by sample size. We present a systematic search for early-time excess emission in a volume-limited sample ($z<0.06$) of SNe~Ia based on the Zwicky Transient Facility Data Release 2 (ZTF DR2). Using ZTF $g$- and $r$-band light curves, we identify candidates showing early-excesses shortly after the explosion time, and we apply conservative coverage and quality requirements to build reliable ``excess'' and ``no-excess'' bump and no-bump catalogs. From an initial sample of 1547 SNe~Ia, our final catalogs contain 42 early-excess and 110 no-excess events. We compare the two populations using SN and host environment parameters from ZTF DR2 and quantify the differences using two-sample statistical tests. We find the strongest differences are in SN light-curve properties: early-excess events have larger SALT2 stretch $x_1$ ($7.91σ$) and larger $r$-band secondary-maximum flux $\mathcal{F}_{r_2}$ ($6.25σ$), while differences in SALT2 color $c$ are weak ($0.57σ$). Early-excess events also favor bluer $(g-z)_{\rm local}$ ($3.41σ$) and lower $\log_{10} (M_*/M_\odot)_{\rm local}$ ($2.73σ$). Our results connect early excesses with SNe~Ia diversity, and motivate further analyses of upcoming larger samples.

### [13] [GeV emission around SS 433 with 17 years Fermi-LAT observation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14623)
*Qiwang Sun,Dmitry Khangulyan,Jiren Liu,Siming Liu*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: Analysis of 17 years of Fermi-LAT data on microquasar SS 433 reveals four GeV sources, including two lobe-associated features (East and West excesses) with distinct spectral properties. The West excess and J1913+0512 are consistent with GeV particles interacting with dense gas. Results suggest first evidence for cosmic-ray proton acceleration in microquasar outflows.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate gamma-ray emission from the microquasar SS 433 and its surrounding nebula W50 over a long timescale (17 years), and to search for evidence of particle acceleration, particularly cosmic-ray protons, in the large-scale outflows of a Galactic microquasar.

Method: Analysis of 17 years of Fermi-LAT observations, including source detection, morphological characterization, spectral fitting, and periodicity search of GeV sources associated with SS 433/W50.

Result: Four GeV sources detected: PS J1910+0550 (new, outside W50), J1913+0512 (previously reported), and East and West excesses associated with X-ray lobes. No periodic modulation from J1913+0512 confirmed. East excess: hard spectrum (photon index ~1.7), consistent with inverse Compton. West excess: soft spectrum (photon index ~2.6), spatially offset from X-ray/TeV emission, similar to J1913+0512.

Conclusion: Both proton-proton and bremsstrahlung scenarios can explain the GeV emission, but the hadronic (pp) scenario is more naturally accommodated. These findings may represent the first observational evidence for cosmic-ray proton acceleration in large-scale outflows from Galactic microquasars.

Abstract: We present an analysis of 17 years of Fermi-LAT observations of the microquasar SS~433. We detect four GeV sources in the region: a newly identified source, PS J1910+0550, located outside W50; the previously reported source J1913+0512; and two features, denoted as the East and West excesses, apparently associated with the X-ray lobes. We focus on the three sources located within W50.
  We do not confirm the previously reported periodic modulation from J1913+0512, as no significant periodicity is found in the full 17-year dataset. The East and West excesses exhibit distinct morphological and spectral properties, suggesting different physical origins. The East excess shows a hard spectrum with photon index $\sim1.7$, consistent with inverse Compton emission from relativistic electrons accelerated together with the particles responsible for the X-ray and TeV emission. In contrast, the West excess has a much softer spectrum with photon index $\sim2.6$ and is spatially offset from the known X-ray and TeV emission regions in the western lobe. The spectral shape and offset position of the West excess make it strikingly similar to J1913+0512.
  The emission from these two regions can be explained by GeV particles accelerated in SS~433, distributed throughout the source volume, and interacting with localized dense gas targets. Under reasonable assumptions regarding particle transport and energetics, both proton-proton and bremsstrahlung scenarios are viable, although the hadronic scenario is more naturally accommodated. These findings may therefore represent the first observational evidence for the acceleration of cosmic-ray protons in large-scale outflows from Galactic microquasars.
