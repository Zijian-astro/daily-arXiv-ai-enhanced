### [1] [The limits of feedback from active galactic nuclei](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16483)
*Andrew Pontzen,Hiranya V. Peiris,Joop Schaye,Matthieu Schaller*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Using FLAMINGO simulations, the study explains why AGN feedback depletes gas in galaxy groups but not in clusters, identifying a ceiling entropy (~360 keV cm²) for outflows that depends on halo mass. Groups below ~10^13.7 M⊙ allow outflows to escape, while clusters above this stall due to higher inflow entropy, leading to gas retention and near-cosmic baryon fractions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To resolve the observed dichotomy where AGN feedback efficiently removes gas from galaxy groups but fails to do so in clusters, which has implications for baryon cycling and galaxy formation.

Method: Analysis of FLAMINGO cosmological simulations, delineating three radial zones (inner shock, intermediate buoyancy, outer termination). Uses Rankine-Hugoniot relations to derive entropy ceiling and critical halo mass. Models time evolution of virial gas fraction.

Result: Outflows have a nearly halo-mass-independent ceiling entropy of ~360 keV cm². Outflows escape halos only when ceiling entropy exceeds inflow entropy, which occurs for M200m < 10^13.7 M⊙. Critical mass shifts with feedback strength (10^13.5–10^14.0 M⊙). Virial gas fractions rise with mass above 10^13.0 M⊙ due to reincorporation.

Conclusion: The entropy ceiling mechanism explains why groups are gas-depleted while clusters retain near-cosmic baryon fractions, governed by the balance between outflow entropy and virial inflow entropy.

Abstract: We use FLAMINGO to investigate why feedback from active galactic nuclei (AGN) significantly depletes gas in galaxy groups but is ineffective in clusters. We delineate three radial zones: an inner zone where AGN feedback heats halo gas via shocks; an intermediate buoyancy zone where the heated halo gas rises; and an outer zone where the outflow may stall in a termination shock. Heating in the inner zone self-limits because, once the gas is sufficiently hot, shocks become too weak to deposit further entropy. Consequently, outflows have a ceiling entropy value ($360\, {\rm keV\, cm^2}$) that is nearly independent of halo mass. These values (and trends with redshift and feedback variants) are explained using an argument based on the Rankine-Hugoniot relations. Outflows rise at fixed entropy through the buoyancy zone, escaping the halo if the ceiling value is sufficiently elevated over that of the inflowing gas. This condition is satisfied only for halo masses $M_{\rm 200m}<10^{13.7}\,{\rm M_\odot}$, because inflow entropy tracks the virial relation. Variants with stronger (or weaker) feedback have a higher (or lower) entropy ceiling and a correspondingly modified critical mass of $M_{\rm 200m}=10^{14.0}\,{\rm M_\odot}$ (or $10^{13.5}\,{\rm M_\odot}$). In clusters above the critical mass, the increased inflow entropy causes the outflow to stall and potentially shock at the 'splashback' radius. We derive an expression for the time evolution of the virial gas fraction, which shows how lingering gas is reincorporated as the halo virial radius expands. This effect dominates over outflows unless they rejoin the Hubble flow; as a result, virial gas fractions rise as a function of mass starting at $M_{\rm 200m} = 10^{13.0}\,{\rm M_\odot}$. These effects explain why groups have depleted gas, while clusters have close to the cosmic baryon fraction.
